Try InkTally with your own class

InkTally marks your students' handwritten work from photos, on your own phone or laptop, and works even without data. This trial takes about an hour across one or two lessons. Your honest notes, good or bad, are the most useful part.

Getting started
  1. Open https://redpen-hybrid.web.app in Chrome on your phone or laptop.
  2. Type the access code redpen-3015d8 and tap Enter.
  3. Tap Save my work and sign in with Google, so your classes are kept if you change phones. (You can skip this; your work then stays on this device only.)
  4. Android phone? You can also use the InkTally app from the link you were sent. It works the same way.

Four short tasks

1
Quick mark one page · 5 minutes
  1. Tap Quick mark. Type the subject and class.
  2. Tap Photograph the page and take a photo of one student's page that has the questions and their answers on it.
  3. InkTally writes an answer key from the questions. Check it and fix anything wrong, then tap Looks right — mark it.
2
Set up a real class exercise · 10 minutes
  1. Tap New assignment. Give it a title, class and subject.
  2. Add a photo of your question paper and tap Draft marking scheme. Already have a marking scheme? Tap Already have a marking scheme?, add it, and tap Use my marking scheme. InkTally copies it exactly, including other accepted answers and mark allocations.
  3. Check the answer key, then tap Save and print answer sheets.
  4. Choose how students will answer:
    • Printed InkTally sheet: print the sheets. Names never leave your device, and marking works fully offline.
    • Exercise book page: no printer needed. Students write in their books as usual. This works best with data on.
3
Mark the whole stack · as long as it takes. Please time it.
  1. Open the assignment and tap Mark.
  2. Either photograph each script (Photograph answer sheet), or take the photos first with your camera and add them all at once with Choose photos.
  3. Each mark shows where it came from:
    On-deviceCloudMarked by codeProvisionalVerified
    "Provisional" means your phone read the answer without data. When you are back online, InkTally checks it again in the cloud by itself.
  4. Open Review. It lists any answers InkTally is unsure about, with the student's handwriting beside what it read. Set the score. If an answer you didn't expect is fine, accept it for everyone and InkTally re-marks the class.
4
Hand back and record · 5 minutes
  1. Open a student's Marked copy. It is their page with red ticks and crosses, the total, and your comment. Share or print it.
  2. Open Mark register and tap Download Excel (.xlsx).
  3. Optional: look at Insights to see which questions the class found hard, and make a catch-up sheet.
Optional: try it with no data
  1. Open the assignment once while online.
  2. Turn mobile data and Wi-Fi off. Reload the page, or close and reopen the app. InkTally still opens.
  3. Add a few scripts with Choose photos, then turn data back on. Watch them get marked.

Your students' privacy

  • Use student numbers or first names only. Don't photograph faces.
  • With printed InkTally sheets, only the cut-out answer boxes are read. The name line and the full photo stay on your device. If your phone has no on-device AI, the answer boxes alone (never names) go to the cloud.
  • With Quick mark or exercise book pages, the whole page photo goes to the cloud so it can be read. Make sure no full names are on the page, or cover them.
  • "The cloud" here means Google's Gemini AI, reached through Firebase. During this trial InkTally uses Google's free tier, where Google may use what is sent to improve its AI, and Google staff may review it (Gemini API terms). That is why printed sheets and student numbers are the safest way to take part.
  • If your school needs permission for trying new teaching tools, please ask your head teacher first.
  • You can delete any assignment and all of its marks at any time: open it and tap Delete.

Thank you. InkTally is being built for teachers with big classes and patchy data, and what you find in your classroom decides what gets fixed next.
